# Discovering Tattoo Artists in the Gallery

## Intent

Digital Wounds is a way to discover tattoo artists. You walk through a picture gallery, a painting catches your eye, and you press E to meet the artist behind it and turn through their designs.

The gallery itself already works (`assets/rooms/museum.json`). What's missing is the reason to walk it: today its paintings are part of the building's texture, soft up close, and not connected to anyone.

Success for this version: every painting on the walls belongs to an artist, none of the old paintings is still visible, and someone can walk up to any of them and page through that artist's designs on sharp images. Adding a real artist or reassigning a painting later takes no code.

## Goals

- Every painting on the walls, roughly 120 counted by eye, is covered by a sharp piece owned by a tattoo artist.
- One artist can own several paintings, so about 20 artists can fill the hall and their work is spread around it. This was the user's decision.
- Walking up to and looking at any painting offers "Press E to see the artist".
- Pressing E opens a full-screen view of that artist, one large design at a time, starting on the design shown by the painting you chose.
- Stand-in artists for now, easy to replace with real ones.
- Sharp rendering on Retina displays, since the art is the point.

## Non-goals

These are out of this version on purpose. The design shouldn't block them, and shouldn't build for them either.

- Contact details, social links, booking.
- Search, filters, or a list of artists.
- Tracking who you've discovered, or any saved progress.
- Replacing the Sketchfab building or changing its licence situation.
- Windows testing (it's still owed before merging to `main`, but it isn't part of this work).

## What would make this a failure

- Paintings that are still blurry when you stand in front of them.
- Any old painting still showing, or a painting that does nothing when you look at it and press E.
- Pressing E opening a different painting's artist than the one you're looking at, which is a real risk because the frames are small and packed close together.
- Text that overflows or gets cut off on the artist screen.
- Stand-in content that reads as real artists: invented names must be clearly fictional, and no real person's name or work is used.
- Adding an artist requires changing C++.

## The experience

**In the gallery.** The walls look as full as they do now, but every piece shows one design by the artist who owns it, in a frame hung exactly over an old painting so that painting is fully hidden. An artist's pieces are spread around the hall rather than grouped, so you start to recognise a style as you walk. When you're close and looking at one, its frame glows slightly and the prompt reads "Press E to see the artist".

**The artist view.** This is option B in `2026-11-06-artist-discovery-mockup.html`, next to this document, chosen by the user:

- Left: one large design, taking about 46% of the screen width, on a light background like a sheet of paper.
- Right, vertically centred: the artist's name in small spaced capitals, the artist's one-line description, the design's title large, the design's description, and a page number such as "2 / 4".
- Bottom: "A/D: Turn page   Q: Close".

The artist's description wasn't in the mockup. It's there so you meet the artist rather than only reading a name. Both descriptions need a length limit that's checked when the data loads, so they can't overflow the column.

**Controls.** A and D turn pages and Q returns to the room. You can't walk while the view is open. Esc behaves as it does in books today.

## Stand-in content

- About 20 artists with about 6 designs each, so every painting shows a different design and the numbers cover all the frames. The counts can vary per artist.
- Dark Ink Studio is one, starting from the name, description and four designs in `public/data/artists/dark-ink/config.json`: The Raven's Eye, Bone Garden, Serpent's Embrace, Shadow Forest. Its remaining designs are invented.
- The others are invented, each with a distinct tattoo style so the differences are visible while walking. Their names should be obviously made up. There's no on-screen "stand-in" label, and Dark Ink's name and text stay as they are because they're the user's own earlier content.
- Each artist has a description, and each design has a title and a description.
- Designs are simple black-ink placeholder drawings generated by a script, so they can be regenerated. They need to look like tattoo flash, not like blank boxes.

## How it's made (decided)

The project stays on its existing stack: C++17, SDL2, OpenGL 3.3, JSON room files. No new dependencies in the game itself.

- **Adding an artist needs no code.** Each artist is a data file plus a folder of images under `assets/artists/<artist-id>/`.
- **Paintings are data.** A list records each painting's position, size and wall, plus which artist and design it shows, so reassigning one is a one-line change. With about 120 entries this probably belongs in its own file rather than inside `assets/rooms/museum.json`.
- **Paintings are found from the model's texture.** The walls are flat rectangles with known texture coordinates, so a rectangle marked on the texture converts directly to a position on a wall. The rectangles need to be recorded carefully, and the result checked on screen wall by wall.
- **Text uses the game's existing pixel font** (`UIOverlay::drawText`), not the serif in the mockup. A finer font was offered as an alternative and not requested.
- **Build on the book viewer.** `src/world/Book.*` already does the open and close animation, page turning, input blocking and sounds, and props already link to books through `book_index`.

## Facts the builder needs

These come from reading the code and aren't obvious.

- **The museum has no lights.** `ambient` and `light_color` are zero and there are no point lights, because the building's look is baked into its emissive texture. A normally lit prop would render black, so the paintings need an unlit or emissive path that shows their true colours at a brightness matching the room.
- **No colour conversion on output.** Textures flagged sRGB are converted to linear, but `postprocess.frag` doesn't convert back. That's why the building needs a tint of 2.0. Decide deliberately how the art is loaded and check it against the source image on screen.
- **Image flipping.** `Texture::load` takes `flipVertically`: 3D surfaces want the default, and the UI needs `false`.
- **Eye height is 5.0 units** because the model is about 3× oversized. The rope posts are about 3 units tall and the wall is 10.9 units high, and paintings stay wherever the old ones were, including the top rows. Those need to be selectable by looking up from the floor.
- **Walls, from the model's floor corners:** x = -10; x = 13.06 for z < -7.49; x = 10.72 for z between -7.49 and 2.08; x = 12.65 for z > 2.08; z = -33.24; z = 30.66. There are no interior walls. The player is stopped 0.5 units short of the outer walls, or 0.3 at the two right-hand wall sections, and can't reach the black doorway on the west wall.
- **Look-at check.** Book interaction requires looking within 30° of the book's position, using its `position` and `interact_radius`, and it takes the first match in the list. That can't work with frames this close together: it has to pick the painting closest to the centre of your view, ideally the one the crosshair is on. With the eye at 5.0, the radius has to allow for that height and for the top rows.
- **One artist, many paintings.** Each `Book` loads its own page textures today. Paintings sharing an artist must share loaded images, and 120 paintings must not load 120 copies of a book. Startup time and memory need checking.
- **The texture has a few non-painting items.** Stanchions, chairs and the black doorway stay as they are. Only framed paintings get covered.
- **Glow.** The frame highlight in `mesh.frag` runs through `uHighlight` when a prop's `book_index` matches the nearby book.
- **Text has no wrapping.** `drawText` draws one line at 8 × scale pixels per character, so descriptions need wrapping.
- **The font is limited.** It only covers ASCII 32 to 122, `&` is blank, and accented letters draw as gaps. Stand-ins stay within what it can draw, and loading warns about any character it can't. Accented names are a known follow-up that must be done before the first real artist who needs one.
- **`player_spawn` is ignored for the first room**, and the player starts at (0, 0, 0).
- **Testing input.** Real keyboard and mouse input reaches the game window during automated runs, so a test harness has to ignore it.

## Retina sharpness

The game currently renders at 1280×720 and macOS scales it up. It should render at the display's real resolution, keeping the UI the same physical size, with resizing still working. This comes first because everything else is judged on it.

Two things to watch: the window size and the pixel size differ once this is on, and the code currently uses `m_windowWidth` and `m_windowHeight` for both the viewport and UI layout. Performance needs checking too, since it's about 4× the pixels with bloom and SSAO. Target: stays at about 60 fps on the user's Apple M5.

## Left to the builder

- Frame look, as long as each frame fully hides the old painting beneath it.
- Which artist gets which painting, as long as each artist's work is spread around.
- How designs fit frames of different shapes. The old paintings vary from tall to wide, so a stand-in can be drawn to fit or sit on a paper mat, but it must not be stretched.
- Data file format and field names, and whether artists extend `Book` or get a sibling class.
- The invented artists, their styles, and the drawing script.
- Image resolution of the stand-ins, as long as they're sharp at the distance you can stand from them and memory stays reasonable.
- Whether to reuse existing book sounds.

## Calls made without asking the user

- About 20 artists with about 6 designs each is a sizing estimate. Only "one artist can own several paintings" and "every painting belongs to an artist" came from the user.
- Pressing E opens on the design shown by the painting you chose, then you can page through the artist's other designs.
- The prompt wording and page layout follow the chat description.
- Retina comes first, as already agreed.
- The artist's description appears on every page, under their name.
- No on-screen "stand-in" label, and invented names aren't checked against real studios.
- Accented names wait until a real artist needs one, with a load-time warning until then.
- Paintings hang in front of the model's wall art rather than replacing the texture, because the 1024-pixel atlas can't hold sharp images.
